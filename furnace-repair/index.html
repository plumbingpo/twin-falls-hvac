From the hallway, a furnace that will not heat looks the same no matter what broke. Inside the cabinet, though, the sequence is strict: inducer on, pressure switch closed, igniter glowing, gas valve open, burners lit, flame proven, and finally the blower sending heat through the house. Diagnosis is mostly a matter of finding where that chain breaks. In Twin Falls homes, it tends to be the same handful of culprits: a flame sensor coated in oxide, a hot surface igniter cracked by thermal cycling, a pressure switch held open by a tired inducer or a plugged condensate trap, or a control board with a burned relay.

Our experienced technicians diagnose a furnace the way it operates, in order, measuring each stage as it happens. That means igniter resistance, microamp flame signal, pressure switch operation under actual inducer draft, gas valve voltage, and blower and capacitor condition. Safety checks are never optional on a gas appliance: heat exchanger inspection, leak testing on the gas connections, and flue draft verification, since a hazard rarely shows itself at the thermostat. We finish by checking temperature rise against the data plate, making sure the burner and blower are matched the way the manufacturer designed them to.

Conventional or condensing, gas, propane, or electric, we repair them all, across every major brand, including the traps, drains, and dual pressure switches that come with high-efficiency models. The parts most likely to fail are stocked on every truck: igniters, flame sensors, pressure switches, capacitors, inducer assemblies, and blower motors. That is why most repairs are finished in a single trip. If your furnace is down anywhere in Twin Falls, call (208) 208-1936 and we will get the burners lit today.

Warning Signs for Furnace Repair

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Burners light, run a few seconds, then shut down repeatedly
  • Water, rust stains, or black soot around the flue or cabinet
  • A fan that keeps running while the registers blow cool air
  • A furnace that shuts off every few minutes before the house warms up
  • Yellow, wavering burner flames where they should be sharp blue
  • Clicking and a glowing igniter with no burner flame
  • Grinding or high-pitched squeals from the blower
  • Booming or banging at light-off from delayed ignition

Early Action Saves Money

A failing capacitor, clogged condensate drain, or dirty flame sensor rarely fixes itself and usually gets more expensive to address the longer it runs. If you notice any of these signs, contact Twin Falls HVAC at (208) 208-1936 for a prompt inspection.

The Benefits of Professional Furnace Repair

The failure that matters most in a furnace is usually hidden. A cracked or corroded heat exchanger can let combustion gases into the airstream, and nothing about it is visible from the outside of the cabinet. Delayed ignition makes things worse, because each boom at light-off puts more stress on that metal. Add high voltage, a gas valve, and a flue to the same box, and there is little room for guesswork. Diagnosis is full of traps too. A high limit that keeps tripping could be a bad switch, or it could be doing exactly its job because a filter is clogged or the blower is dying, and swapping out a working switch only hides the real fault.

At Twin Falls HVAC, no symptom gets treated in isolation. A meter tells us the flame signal, so we never condemn a good sensor while the real culprit, a cracked igniter or a weak gas valve, stays in place. We confirm draft at both the pressure switch and the flue, measure temperature rise against the data plate to flag airflow problems early, and leak-test every gas connection with instruments, not a guess. When we are done, the furnace has proven itself safe and correct through a full cycle, and if it is nearing the end of its useful life, you will get an honest assessment.

Maintenance Tips for Furnace Repair

A little routine maintenance goes a long way toward avoiding a mid-season breakdown. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Have the furnace checked every autumn, while a weak igniter or fouled sensor is still an easy fix
  • Notice how the furnace sounds at startup, because a quiet light-off is healthy and a boom is not
  • Give the furnace room to breathe, since a crowded utility space starves the burners of air
  • Put in a fresh filter monthly through the heating season, the cheapest protection your furnace can get
  • On high-efficiency models, keep the condensate trap and drain line clear and draining
  • Test your CO detectors monthly and make sure each floor has one
  • Store laundry supplies, especially chlorine products, well away from the furnace closet
